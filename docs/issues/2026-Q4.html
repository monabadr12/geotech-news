Before you use a storylead → primary source → own words → short quotes → image rights
  1. Treat the news item as a lead, not as your source.
  2. Check the facts with the primary source: the authority, the project owner, the company's press release or the official report (“Check with”, where the news desk found it).
  3. Write the piece in your own words, with your own angle: the magazine angle is a starting point.
  4. Quote a sentence or two at most, in quotation marks, naming the publication and linking to it.
  5. For images, use your own, the project's press-kit photos released for media use, or ask permission; never reuse a source's photos, figures or drawings.

Proposed line-up for Q4 2026

Drafted by Claude on 24 Oct 2026 from 60 stories and 20 papers · check every story at its source before publishing

So far this quarter's news is dominated by rain. Monsoon and autumn storms have triggered slope failures in residual soils, tuff and road cuts across Vietnam, Italy, Chile and Indonesia, and caused urban sinkholes in Mexico, Chile, Ecuador and South Korea, where leaking pipes, poor backfill and drainage lie behind collapses that swallowed cars and, in one Mexican case, led to a negligence finding after a death (S57). Two strands go beyond emergencies: retaining-wall failures and an excavation sinkhole that occurred despite a completed underground safety assessment in South Korea are prompting questions of accountability, and energy-related underground works are under pressure, with further cost rises at Snowy 2.0 and a patent for underground hydrogen storage. Unusually, the most widely syndicated item is a scientific discovery rather than a failure: a giant karst doline in Phong Nha-Ke Bang, reported in at least five language editions. All 60 stories were collected over just three days (22–24 October). Many are translated duplicates of Vietnam.vn items, and the Google News editions are a poor guide to where stories happened, so the balance should be refreshed before the meeting.
