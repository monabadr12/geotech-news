Method & sources

How the digest is made

A scheduled scan reads specialist, institutional and trade sources plus worldwide news searches, filters them for geotechnical substance, and files the result under the magazine's eight themes.

Each run

  1. Collect. 26 websites and 76 Google News searches (8 core searches in 7 English editions, plus 20 further searches including 14 in other languages). Each run looks back 60 hours; stories already shown are remembered for 45 days and never repeated.
  2. Score. Headlines and summaries are scored against 78 weighted geotechnical keywords; specialist and society sources earn bonus points, noise terms lose points, near-identical headlines are merged. A story needs 3 points to go forward.
  3. Triage. Up to 130 candidates go to Claude, which rates each 1–5 for geotechnical substance (below 3 is dropped), assigns a theme and a suggested use, writes a one-sentence magazine angle, and translates non-English headlines.
  4. Publish & archive. Up to 60 stories per digest. Every digest is stored permanently and grouped into three-month issue windows.

Known limits

Coverage of a country depends on what its press publishes online in a language covered here. Google News favours recency and mainstream outlets, so a major landslide is picked up readily while a quiet technical advance may not be — which is why society and institutional feeds are read directly.

Headlines link to the original publishers. Treat every story as a lead and verify with the primary source before publishing. Ratings, themes and angles are machine-assisted suggestions for the editorial team.
