Method & sources

How the digest is made

A scheduled scan reads specialist, institutional and trade sources plus worldwide news searches, filters them for geotechnical substance, and files the result under the magazine's eight themes.

Each run

  1. Collect. 19 websites and 103 Google News searches (8 core searches in 7 English editions, plus 47 further searches including 26 in other languages). The scan runs every day at 05:00 Cairo time, and on demand: Scan now opens the scan's page on GitHub, where you press Run workflow; the website then follows the scan and tells you when the new digest is ready. Each run looks back 60 hours; stories already shown are remembered for 45 days and never repeated.
  2. Score and categorise. Headlines and summaries are scored against 58 English keywords and 951 terms in 16 other languages. Each foreign term counts as its English equivalent (for example glissement de terrain, deslizamiento, longsor and انهيار أرضي all count as “landslide”), so every story is filed under an English theme. Specialist sources earn bonus points, noise terms lose points, near-identical headlines are merged. A story needs 3 points to go forward.
  3. Triage. Up to 130 candidates go to Claude, which rates each 1–5 for geotechnical substance (below 3 is dropped), confirms the theme, suggests a use, writes a one-sentence magazine angle, and translates non-English headlines.
  4. Research. New papers from 25 geotechnical journals, read from Crossref's free index (papers registered in the last 4 days, each shown once). They are scored and themed with the same keywords; Claude rates each 1–5 for its use in practice and writes one sentence on why it matters to practitioners. Up to 20 papers per digest appear under New research, for the Academia-to-Industry pages.
  5. Publish & archive. Up to 60 stories per digest. Every digest is stored permanently, grouped into three-month issue windows, and exported to Excel.
  6. Issue brief. In the last 21 days of each quarter Claude reads the quarter's stories rated 3 / 5 or higher (at most 40 per theme) and any research papers, and drafts the brief for the editorial meeting as a Word document: an overview, a proposed line-up per theme, feature leads with the kinds of experts to approach, Academia-to-Industry topics, a regional-balance check, gaps worth covering and a watch list. The draft is refreshed every 7 days and the final brief is written the day after the quarter closes. Generate / update brief on an issue page starts one on GitHub at any time.

Known limits

Coverage of a country depends on what its press publishes online in a language covered here. Google News favours recency and mainstream outlets, so a major landslide is picked up readily while a quiet technical advance may not be — which is why society and institutional feeds are read directly.

Headlines link to the original publishers. Treat every story as a lead and verify with the primary source before publishing. Ratings, themes and angles are machine-assisted suggestions for the editorial team. Website report (Word)
